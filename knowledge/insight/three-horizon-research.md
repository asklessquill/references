# Research benefits from three time horizons

## Observation
Short-term information is fast but noisy, medium-term evidence reveals clusters and repeated patterns, and long-term history provides structural context.

## Interpretation
A single research cadence tends either to react too quickly or to miss important recent changes.

## Decision relevance
Maintain three complementary layers:
- immediate: what changed recently
- medium: what patterns are forming
- long: what persists across cycles

A useful decision process can compare all three before acting.

## Limits
The correct horizon lengths depend on the domain. Minutes, days, months, or years may all be appropriate in different systems.
