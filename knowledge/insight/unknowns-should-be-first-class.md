# Unknowns should be represented as first-class knowledge

## Observation

Systems that record only conclusions tend to hide uncertainty and repeatedly rediscover missing information.

## Interpretation

Knowing what is not known is operationally useful. Unknowns can drive acquisition, experimentation, and escalation without pretending to be evidence.

## Decision relevance

Track unknowns with provenance, expected decision impact, cheapest resolution path, and expiry or review date. Promote them only when evidence arrives.

## Limits

An unknown registry can become noise if every imaginable uncertainty is captured. Prioritize unknowns that can change decisions.
